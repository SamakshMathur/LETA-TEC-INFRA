[POINT 1/10] **LETA INTERPRETATION OF USER QUERY**
Whether services rendered by an Indian branch office (registered under GST) to its foreign head office (HO) constitute "export of services" qualifying as zero-rated supply under Section 16 of the IGST Act, entitling the Indian branch to refund of unutilised ITC or IGST paid.

---

[POINT 2/10] **MAIN CONCLUSIVE ANSWER (EXECUTIVE SUMMARY)**
**This is a legally contentious position.** The Indian branch and the foreign HO are treated as **distinct/related persons** under GST law. Consequently, the transaction may **fail the "export of services" test** on two critical grounds:
1. **Same legal entity argument** — branch and HO are not separate "persons" in law
2. **Consideration test** — reimbursements from HO may not qualify as "consideration" in a legally enforceable sense

The net position is **NOT straightforwardly an export** and is subject to active judicial scrutiny.

---

[POINT 3/10] **FACTUAL UNDERSTANDING / ASSUMPTIONS**
The following assumptions are made:
- The Indian entity is a **branch office** of a foreign company (not a subsidiary)
- The branch is **registered under GST** in India
- The foreign HO is located **outside India**
- The Indian branch performs services (e.g., back-office, IT, support) and is **reimbursed** by the foreign HO
- The question is whether such reimbursement/income qualifies as **export of services**

[POINT 5/10] **VERBATIM STATUTORY EXTRACTS**

**Section 16(1), IGST Act** (from Verified Citation Registry):
> *"'zero rated supply' means any of the following supplies of goods or services or both, namely:— (a) export of goods or services or both; or (b) supply of goods or services or both to a Special Economic Zone developer or a Special Economic Zone unit."*

**Section 16(3), IGST Act** (from Verified Citation Registry):
> *"A registered person making zero rated supply shall be eligible to claim refund under either of the following options: (a) he may supply goods or services or both under bond or Letter of Undertaking, without payment of integrated tax and claim refund of unutilised input tax credit; or (b) he may supply goods or services or both, subject to such conditions, safeguards and procedure as may be prescribed, on payment of integrated tax and claim refund of such tax paid."*

**Section 7 + Schedule I, CGST Act** (from Verified Citation Registry):
> *"For the purposes of this Act, the expression 'supply' includes... the activities specified in Schedule I, made or agreed to be made without a consideration."*
> *(Schedule I includes: supply between **related persons or distinct persons** in the course or furtherance of business — **even without consideration**)*

**Section 54(1), CGST Act** (from Truth Rules — SECTION_54_REFUND):
> Refund application must be filed **within 2 years** from the relevant date. For zero-rated supplies under LUT, relevant date = **end of the quarter in which export invoice was issued.**

[POINT 6/10] **LEGAL ANALYSIS & ADVERSARIAL REASONING**

### ✅ ARGUMENT IN FAVOUR — Qualifies as Export

**Section 2(6) of IGST Act** defines "export of services" as a supply that satisfies ALL FIVE conditions:

| # | Condition | Status in Branch-HO Scenario |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supplier located in India | ✅ Indian Branch |
| 2 | Recipient located outside India | ✅ Foreign HO |
| 3 | Place of supply is outside India | ⚠️ Contested — see below |
| 4 | Payment received in convertible foreign exchange | ⚠️ Must be verified |
| 5 | Supplier and recipient are **not merely establishments of the same person** | ❌ **Biggest hurdle** |

---

### ❌ ADVERSARIAL CHALLENGE — Fails the "Distinct Person" / "Same Legal Entity" Test

**Explanation to Section 2(6) of the IGST Act** — This is the decisive provision:

> *"A person carrying on a business through a branch or agency or representational office in any territory shall be treated as having an establishment in that territory."*

Further, the **Explanation to Section 8 of the IGST Act** provides:

> *"Where a person has an establishment in India and any other establishment outside India... such establishments shall be treated as establishments of distinct persons."*

**Critical Legal Consequence:**
- The Indian branch and the foreign HO are treated as **establishments of distinct persons**
- Therefore, **Condition 5 of Section 2(6)** — that supplier and recipient must NOT be mere establishments of the same person — is **violated**
- This means the supply **cannot qualify as export of services**

---

### ⚠️ PLACE OF SUPPLY COMPLICATION

Under **Section 13 of the IGST Act** (cross-border services), the general rule is:
> *"The place of supply of services... shall be the location of the recipient of services."*

However, since branch and HO are treated as **establishments of the same person (distinct persons)**, the IGST Act's own framework treats this as an **intra-entity supply** — making the cross-border POS rule potentially inapplicable.

---

### 🏛️ JUDICIAL POSITION

The **Gujarat High Court** in **C/SCA/1350/2021** (from Retrieved Source Documents) confirmed the principle that taxing statutes must be interpreted strictly and courts are reluctant to read down formulae or provisions where the legislative intent is clear:
> *"...courts are averse to entering the area of policy matters on fiscal issues."*

This reinforces that the statutory language of Section 2(6) read with its Explanation must be applied as written — and the branch-HO relationship **squarely falls within the "same establishment" exclusion.**

[POINT 10/10] **FINAL TAX POSITION & CAVEATS**

**FINAL POSITION:**

> ❌ **Services by Indian branch to foreign HO do NOT ordinarily qualify as "export of services"** under Section 2(6) of the IGST Act, because the Indian branch and foreign HO are treated as **establishments of the same person (distinct persons)** — this violates the fifth condition of export of services.

**Consequences:**
1. **LUT cannot be validly filed** for such transactions
2. **ITC refund under zero-rated route** is not available
3. The supply may be treated as a **taxable intra-entity supply** under Schedule I of CGST Act — GST may be payable by the Indian branch
4. If foreign exchange is received and proper invoicing is done, the taxpayer may attempt to characterise it as export — but this is a **litigious position** and department is likely to contest

**Caveats:**
- This opinion is based on the statutory provisions and retrieved documents. The CGST Act, IGST Act definitions (Sections 2(6), 8, 13), and Schedule I form the backbone of this analysis
- **CBIC Circular No. 210/4/2024-GST** specifically addresses HO-Branch transactions — **mandatory verification from official CBIC source is required**
- The taxpayer must obtain a **formal legal opinion** and ideally an **Advance Ruling** before filing LUT or claiming refund in such transactions
- Tax treatment may vary based on the **specific nature of services** rendered and whether any cost allocation or markup is involved
